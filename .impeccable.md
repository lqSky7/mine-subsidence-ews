## Impeccable UI: Uber-Inspired Operator Dashboard

### Users
Hackathon demonstrators and mine-control operators use the dashboard to spot changing sensor conditions, understand why an early warning was raised, and decide whether to inspect the test rig.

### Design Goal
Make the product feel like an engineering tool built by a team with Uber/Base discipline: stark, fast to scan, grounded in reusable primitives, and free from decorative noise. The interface should feel decisive without becoming theatrical.

### Uber Design Guidelines
- Use true black `#000000` and pure white `#ffffff` as the primary system. Neutral grays are for surfaces, dividers, and secondary text only.
- Keep the UI chrome achromatic. Amber and red are reserved for safety semantics: watch and critical. Green is used sparingly for live/nominal state.
- Use the system sans stack as an UberMove substitute. Headings are direct and compact; labels are medium weight and sentence case where possible.
- Controls are pills. Buttons, filters, chips, and nav states use full radius. Content cards stay compact with 8px radius.
- Prefer flat surfaces and hairline borders over colored cards, heavy shadows, gradients, and decorative icon blocks.
- Organize telemetry as progressive disclosure: first show risk, live state, and the few readings that change decisions; move supporting detail into tables, links, or compact rows.
- Replace long marketing or AI language with operator nouns: "Risk", "Evidence", "Gas", "Tilt", "Wall", "Vibration", "Action".
- Use an 8px spacing rhythm. Dense is fine; crowded is not. Every row must have a single job.
- Do not depend on color alone. Pair status color with labels, state text, and shape.
- Avoid nested cards. Sections are full-width layouts; cards are only for repeated items, tool panels, or framed data modules.

### Component Rules
- Primary action: black pill, white text, 32-36px height.
- Secondary action: white pill, black text, 1px black or neutral border.
- Destructive action: red is allowed only for manual alarm, critical acknowledgement, or emergency broadcast.
- Status badges: uppercase, tiny, pill-shaped, with neutral default and semantic warning/critical variants.
- Tables: compact rows, sticky logic by visual grouping only, no paragraph-heavy cells.
- Empty states: short title, one plain sentence, no illustrations unless they explain a physical state.

### Product Principles
- Put signal, confidence, and contributing sensors before generic AI language.
- Escalate calmly: stable, watch, then critical.
- Make the model's limits visible: this is an anomaly indicator, not a subsidence confirmation.
- Preserve scan-friendly operator layouts across desktop and mobile.
